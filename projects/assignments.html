Project 1 — Supporting Work

Nostalgia Time Capsule

Creating my nostalgia time capsule helped me better understand my strongest feelings of nostalgia. Looking at photos of my favorite amusement park from childhood immediately brought back memories of a simpler, happier time. That place represents pure childhood memories and a carefree sense of happiness, which I deeply miss.

I also feel a strong fondness for my high school friends. The photo of us together reminds me of shared laughter and a feeling of belonging. Even now, thinking about those friendships brings warmth and emotional comfort. Traveling with friends in Nanjing, China was another meaningful experience that I often reflect on. Those trips strengthened our bond and created memories that still evoke nostalgia today.

My hometown, Wuhan, holds a special place in my heart. The sunset by the river reminds me of my roots. Riding a bicycle to school every day was an ordinary routine. These small moments now feel especially precious.

Finally, images of Chinese New Year's Eve dinner with my family represent collective nostalgia. Gathering together, sharing food, and celebrating traditions gave me a strong sense of connection. The rainbow I saw in high school still makes me feel optimistic. It reminds me that nostalgia is also about hope for the future. This time capsule shows how revisiting these memories restores my enthusiasm and optimism as I move forward in life.

Project 2 — Supporting Work

Anatomy of an Introduction

An exercise dissecting the anatomy of an introductory paragraph for a two-author analytical essay. Using a provided example, I organized my own introduction for the Drawing Connections project following four structural steps:

1. Attention Grabber In recent years, Disney's live-action remakes have consistently dominated the global box office, but they have also consistently sparked heated debates among audiences and critics.
2. Introduce Texts The two main texts analyzed are Richard Newby's article "Lion King, Originality and the Backlash Against Remakes" and Nerdstalgic's YouTube video essay "The Problem With Disney's Movies In One Word."
3. Lead to Main Idea An analysis of these two perspectives reveals that the intense backlash against Disney's remakes stems from far more than a mere lack of originality.
4. Thesis Statement The backlash represents a complex, multifaceted reaction — a resistance to careless creative execution, a warning against the toxic double-edged sword of nostalgia, and a growing concern about corporate monopolization of shared cultural narratives.

Project 2 — Supporting Work

Analytical Essay Outline

A structured outline building the skeleton of the Drawing Connections Essay — organizing thesis, topic sentences, evidence, and analysis for each body paragraph.

Body Paragraph 1 — Originality vs. Adaptation

Topic Sentence: Many critics complain that remaking films like The Lion King or Aladdin proves Hollywood has run out of new ideas. However, adaptation is a longstanding human storytelling tradition, and Disney's foundational success was built entirely on remaking public domain fairy tales.

Newby: "The vast majority of Disney's animated classics are already retellings. The studio's cinematic success that began with Snow White and the Seven Dwarves (1937) was founded on the idea of remaking."
Nerdstalgic: Intertextual moments "shouldn't be relied on to make up for the lack of storytelling creativity. It's almost as if Disney believes we aren't smart enough or capable enough of understanding a new story" (5:55–6:05).

Connection: While Newby points out that retelling stories is a historical norm, Nerdstalgic argues that Disney's current method leans too heavily on mere visual recognition rather than actual creative effort. Together, they suggest audiences reject the lazy execution, not the concept of remakes itself.

Body Paragraph 2 — The Double-Edged Sword of Nostalgia

Topic Sentence: Audiences are drawn to remakes out of a deep desire to revisit childhood favorites. Yet any deviation from the original memory often sparks immediate and intense outrage.

Newby: "There is unquestionably an element of nostalgia that drives audiences to theaters… and that element is largely the reason behind why casting decisions so often turn ugly."
Nerdstalgic: "We begin to seek out the nostalgia and resent everything that isn't" (4:19–4:24).

Connection: Both authors agree that nostalgia is a double-edged sword: it guarantees massive ticket sales while simultaneously breeding a rigid, toxic fan environment that stifles progressive artistic choices.

Body Paragraph 3 — Corporate Monopoly of Cultural Narratives

Topic Sentence: Disney's specific versions have become the most recognized worldwide. Critics worry a single corporation is erasing the broader history of global stories.

Newby: "These remakes are now the dominant tellings of these stories, while the knowledge that they are only a small part of history has become lost on too many moviegoers."
Nerdstalgic: "We've grown up. We know these moments too well to be fooled by the moment they're recreating… Maybe it's time Disney grows with us" (8:31–8:44).

Connection: Newby warns Disney's monopolistic approach threatens to rewrite storytelling history. Nerdstalgic builds on this by arguing the dominance relies on "fooling" an audience that has already outgrown these mechanical recreations.

Project 3 — Supporting Work

Research & Selection of Primary Text

A research log documenting the process of finding and selecting a primary academic text for the Source Assessment project.

Research Proposal

Researching escapism within the field of Digital Media and Communication — specifically how game mechanics and software design facilitate "active" escapism and its impact on user self-perception, including the boundary between healthy immersion and addiction.

Search Keywords

  • Active escapism
  • Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)
  • Game mechanics / Game design
  • Healthy immersion
  • Video game addiction
  • Player self-regulation
  • Simulated worlds

Sources Evaluated

Source 1 "Exploring Future Directions for Healthy Escapism and Self-Regulation in Games" — Explores how system design can interfere with or facilitate a player's ability to regulate their gaming behavior.
Source 2 "Fanboys, competers, escapists and time-killers: a typology based on gamers' motivations" — Categorizes gamers based on motivations, identifying an "escapist gamer" cluster who utilize games for freedom.
Source 3 ✓ "Continuance Intention and Addiction: Analyzing the Role of Escapism in Balanced Gaming Engagement" — Differentiates between healthy continued engagement and game addiction, highlighting escapism as a strong predictor of addiction.

Why Source 3 Was Chosen

This paper directly addresses the core conflict: the developer's dilemma of balancing sustained player engagement with the ethical risks of game addiction. Unlike the other sources, it uses quantitative data to suggest actionable, responsible game design strategies — making it the perfect foundation for a Computer Science and HCI-focused research project.

Project 3 — Supporting Work

Primary Text Source Assessment Chart

The detailed source assessment chart for the primary text used in Writing Project #3.

Title & Author "Continuance Intention and Addiction: Analyzing the Role of Escapism in Balanced Gaming Engagement" — Mengyuan Li, Wendy Wan Yee Hui, and Torsten Reiners
Publication 2025 IEEE International Conference on Service Operations and Logistics, and Informatics (SOLI). Peer-reviewed academic conference paper.
Discipline Information Systems, Business Management, and Cyberpsychology. Explores the tension between continuance intention and game addiction.
Genre Academic Conference Paper / Empirical Research Study. Uses structured format with statistical modeling and visual diagrams.
Organization Establishes a real-world business and ethical problem, reviews existing theories (TAM, SET), details a survey of 123 Pokémon Go players, presents findings, and concludes with practical implications.

Main Ideas

  • Enjoyment drives both positive and negative outcomes — it's a strong predictor of both continuance intention and game addiction.
  • Escapism is solely linked to addiction — it significantly predicts addiction but does not predict healthy engagement.
  • Ethical game design is possible by promoting enjoyment while reducing escapism-driven engagement.

Reflection

I learned the clear distinction between enjoyment and escapism in gaming. While both keep players playing, escapism is the key factor that pushes behavior from healthy engagement into addiction. In the discipline of Information Systems, business goals must often be carefully balanced against human psychology and ethical design.
